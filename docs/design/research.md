# Company research

_Read before changing `internal/news`, fundamentals, or `/research`._

## Company research

`/research` answers what the *companies* in a book are doing, rather than what the book has done: a merged feed of headlines about every open holding, and each company's reported revenue, net income and diluted EPS.

**This is the only content the system relays rather than derives, and that is the whole design problem.** A price is checked before it is stored — against the currency on file, against the market the instrument trades on — and a position can be replayed from the ledger that produced it. A headline can be checked against nothing. What replaces the check is one rule: **an article is attached to a holding only when the provider itself tagged it with that holding's listing code.** Never by finding the company's name in a title, never by a keyword search. The rule under-reports — the sources leave plenty of articles untagged, so a holding shows fewer headlines than exist — and that is the direction to fail in. A missing headline costs the reader nothing; one attributed to the wrong company is the same class of harm as a fee silently read as zero.

The page says so in a `.notice` rather than leaving a reader to wonder why a busy week looks quiet.

**Three obvious sources were tried and rejected**, all verified live; do not reach for them again:

- **Yahoo's `v1/finance/search?newsCount=N`** — the tempting one, since `quotes/search.go` already calls that endpoint with `newsCount=0`. Its news array is not symbol-scoped: asked about `2330.TW` it answered with Deutsche Telekom, Argan, Lynas and Toyota.
- **cnyes's keyword search** (`ess.api.cnyes.com/.../news/keyword?q=2330`) — three of five results were Ethereum and Bitcoin analysis.
- **Yahoo's `v10/finance/quoteSummary`** — now **401 without a cookie and crumb**. It is where the earnings calendar lives, which is why there is no next-earnings-date anywhere in this system.

What is used instead: Yahoo's per-symbol **RSS** feed for US holdings (genuinely symbol-scoped, English), cnyes's Taiwanese **category firehose** for TWSE/TPEX holdings (Chinese), and Yahoo's **`ws/fundamentals-timeseries`** endpoint for reported figures, which needs no crumb and answers for both markets.

**The firehose is fanned out, not queried per stock.** cnyes has no per-stock endpoint, but every article in the category feed carries a `market` array of tagged symbols (`TWS:2330:STOCK`), so one request covers the whole Taiwanese side of a book — more accurate than a per-stock query and gentler on the provider than N of them. The sibling `stock` field is deliberately ignored: it carries the same codes without naming a venue and was measured to be strictly poorer.

**The venue prefix in a cnyes tag says which country, not which venue**, and getting that wrong is silent. TWS reads as the exchange and TWG as Gretai — the old name of the over-the-counter market — so the obvious rule is to cross-check them against `Instrument.Market`, the way `quotes.FromTicker` cross-checks an exchange against a ticker suffix. Measured against the quote provider, two of three disagree: 2330 is TWSE and tagged `TWS`, but 3374 is TPEX and tagged `TWS`, and 6789 is TWSE and tagged `TWG`. Cross-checking therefore drops correct articles about held companies and reports nothing. The venue is used only to tell a Taiwanese listing from a US one; the **code alone** decides which holding, which is safe because Taiwanese listing codes are unique across both venues. The US prefix stays excluded, since a US holding is served in English by Yahoo and letting a Chinese article attach as well would double-source exactly those holdings and make the language tag meaningless.

**An article is a row, its attributions are a join.** `models.NewsMention` is a join table rather than a copy of the article per instrument, which is a deliberate departure from how `DailyClose` keys its rows. One cnyes piece genuinely names four companies, and the feed has to render **one row carrying every ticker** — which duplicated rows cannot do without grouping them back together on every read. The same article also arrives twice when it is filed under two holdings' Yahoo feeds, so `handlers.newsRows` folds duplicates before writing: one article row, one mention per holding it names.

**Articles are shared; the feed is personal.** `news_items` is master data on the same footing as a price, so the sync endpoint is open to any authenticated user and rate-limited, exactly as `refresh-quotes` is. Which of those articles reaches a reader is decided entirely by what they hold, scoped in the SQL itself — an admin sees no more of it than anybody else. Closed holdings drop out: news about a company sold last year answers nothing a reader can act on, and "If you had never sold" is where that question already lives.

**`FinancialFact` carries the currency the company *reported* in, and a mismatch with the instrument's is not an error.** This is the one place the quote package's currency rule does not carry over, and the distinction is exact: a quote or a price series in the wrong currency is refused because adopting it would reinterpret every cost basis behind it, while these figures are never added to a cost basis or a market value. An ADR reporting in USD against a TWD listing is the ordinary case, so the reported currency is stored as given, always printed beside the figure, and called out in a notice when it differs. `TestReportedCurrencyIsKeptWhenItDiffersFromTheTradingCurrency` pins it down. A currency this system cannot model at all is still refused, since there is nowhere to put it.

**Unknown is not zero here either.** A period the provider has no figure for arrives as null and renders as an em dash — TSMC genuinely has no diluted EPS on file for one quarter — and the bar chart draws **nothing** for it rather than a zero-height bar sitting on the axis, which would read as a quarter the company earned nothing in. The axis names the missing period in grey so the gap says which one it was.

**Bars are measured from zero.** A truncated baseline would turn TSMC's real 36% year into a cliff, which is the oldest way there is to lie with a bar chart. The growth the bars understate is stated outright beside them as a year-on-year percentage, found by *date* — the period ending in the same month a year back — rather than by counting four rows back, which a run with a gap in it would get wrong and report as growth. The change is divided by the magnitude of the base, so a swing from a loss to a profit reads as a gain rather than as its negative.

**One sync button, two cadences.** `POST /research/sync` drives both jobs, because the page has one button and the two have wildly different natural refresh rates — a headline is stale within the hour, a quarterly result is the same number for three months. That is handled by `newsFreshness` (30 minutes) and `fundamentalsFreshness` (24 hours) skipping what was checked recently, not by asking a reader to press twice and know which press does what. A failed source leaves its holdings unstamped so the next press retries them immediately, exactly as a failed quote fetch leaves its timestamps alone. A run is proportional to the **caller's own open holdings** rather than to the master data, and a partial failure is not an error for the run: one source being down must not cost the reader the other's headlines. In practice a first run is about two seconds and a second press within the window costs the providers nothing.

`internal/news` is a separate package from `internal/quotes` because it has two providers where that package's contract claims one; reported figures live in `quotes` since they come from the same provider and the same ticker map. The metric and period-type vocabulary (`models.MetricRevenue`, `models.PeriodQuarter`) lives in `models` because it is what the rows are keyed by — the providers' own names for the same things are translated at their boundary.
