# Allocation

_Read before changing `allocation.ts` or the allocation block on `/positions`._

## Allocation

Every other figure in this system is about profit. `frontend/src/allocation.ts` is about **risk**, and it answers the one question a ledger can answer exactly while a broker statement usually will not: how much of this is in one stock.

It is pure frontend arithmetic over the positions already on the page, unit-tested like the other math modules, because nothing about it needs the database — the weights are market values over a total, and both are already on `PositionView`. `Instrument.AssetType` is carried through onto that view so a book can be sliced by **what it holds** as well as by which venue it sits on; an ETF-heavy book and a stock-picking one are different books on the same market, and an instrument the provider never classified gets its own bucket rather than being folded into either.

Two rules, and they are the ones the rest of the system already follows. The denominator is **one currency's priced market value**, so there is no cross-currency weight — that number does not exist here. And an **unpriced holding is excluded rather than drawn as 0%**, because its value is unknown and it may be the largest position in the book; the count of what was left out comes back on the `Allocation` so the block can say what share of itself the picture accounts for. A concentration understated is the wrong way for this figure to be wrong.

The view fetches **every** open holding rather than allocating over the current page (`loadAllOpen` pages to the end), since a concentrated position sitting on page two would otherwise simply not appear — which is the one case the whole feature exists for. It renders as a single stacked bar rather than a pie: a length is read accurately and an angle is not, and comparing one wedge against another is the entire point.

## Target allocation and the contribution planner

`models.TargetWeight` records what share of its currency's book a user means an instrument to be, in basis points; `GET/PUT /settings/targets` read and replace the set, and the block that edits it (`components/TargetAllocation.vue`) sits below the Holdings table. The arithmetic is `frontend/src/rebalance.ts`, pure and unit-tested like the module above, and it keeps the same two rules: weights are shares of **one currency's priced book**, and an unpriced holding is left out and counted.

**The set is saved whole and each currency must add up to exactly 100%** (`db.SaveTargets`, `ErrInvalidTargets` → 400). A target only means something beside the others in its currency, so weights saved one at a time would pass through states adding up to 130% and the planner would read nonsense from any of them. A currency with no targets at all is simply unplanned; one with 95% planned is refused, because the planner would have to guess where the other 5% was meant to go. An instrument may be targeted before it is held — the view carries its `last_price` so a purchase of it can be sized — and a holding with no target counts toward the book but never receives money: the plan is complete, so anything outside it is meant to shrink as a share, and without selling the only way to do that is to stop buying it.

**Two modes, and Buy only is the default.** *Buy only* never sells: each line's "To buy" is what it needs for every target to be met without a sale — the most overweight line sets how big the plan's part of the book has to grow, since nothing is taken from it — and the planner splits a contribution (below). Holdings outside the plan are left out of that figure and named, because buying can never bring one to its target of zero; the targets are then reached as shares of the rest of the book. *Buy and sell* is the broker's rebalance button: the table is signed, and `planRebalance` lists the trades that land every line on target, with optional new money folded in and lines outside the plan sold out. **Each sale carries the realized result it would stamp**, by the ledger's own moving-average rule (`PositionState.Apply`: cost removed = total cost × fraction sold, rounded half-up, all of it on a full exit) and before fees, because that gain or loss — a taxable event, and a `RealizedPL` stamp — is what selling costs and what Buy only exists to avoid. The default is Buy only for that reason; the choice is remembered per browser, as a viewer convenience. A sold-out line sells exactly the shares held rather than value ÷ price, which floating point can land a hair under the holding.

In whole-share mode a rebalance first rounds every trade toward zero shares, which does not cancel out: a sale of 0.4 of a share is dropped while the buys it was meant to fund are not, and on the real book that left a "plan" needing $405 nobody had offered. So the buys are settled against the cash actually there — shares come off the buy nearest its target until the plan is affordable, and leftover cash buys shares of the largest shortfall, only while a share brings its line closer to target rather than past it.

Neither mode fills the trade form in: an order not yet placed has no fill price, and the trade worth recording is the one the broker confirms.

**Money goes to the largest shortfall in money first**, where a shortfall is weight × (book + contribution) − current value — the drift table's "To target" column, measured against the book the contribution will make. The largest is brought down to the next, then both together, which leaves the smallest worst gap in percentage points, the unit the drift column speaks. Ranking by how *empty* each target is relative to its own size (value ÷ weight) was tried first and rejected on the real book: a new 10% target held at nothing outranked a 35% target $1,100 short, so $300 went to two small lines while the one the table showed furthest behind got nothing. The shortfalls add up to at least the contribution, so no target is ever pushed past its weight, and amounts are rounded to the minor unit with the remainder handed out by largest fraction so they add up exactly. **Whole-share mode** is the same rule made discrete — one share at a time of the affordable target with the largest shortfall — and reports what could not buy another share of anything still below target as left over. A target whose holding has no quote is skipped and named: its current weight is unknown, so whether it is under or over is too.
