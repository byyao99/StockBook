# Splits

_Read before changing split detection or the split warnings._

## Splits

`models.SplitEvent` is the one thing in this system that is stored purely so it can be **warned about**. Splits are out of scope and stay out of scope — correcting a book for one means restating every share count recorded before it, which invalidates the ledger's own claim to be a record of what happened. What changed is that not modelling them used to mean not *noticing* them, and that is a silent failure of exactly the kind everything else here is built to refuse.

**The failure it closes.** The closes in `DailyClose` are stated in post-split shares all the way back, and so is `Instrument.LastPrice`, because that is what the provider serves. The ledger records the shares as they were actually bought. After a 2:1 split those two stop agreeing, and a holding is valued at half what it is worth — with the curve, the drawdown, the benchmark comparison and the unrealized figure on the holdings page all wrong by the ratio and nothing on screen saying so. A Taiwanese book meets this sooner than a US one: a **stock dividend** (股票股利) is reported by the provider as a split, and they are ordinary.

**It costs no extra request,** exactly as the distributions do: `quotes.Client.History` asks for `events=div,split` on the call the price sync already makes. `TestHistoryAsksForDividendsAndSplits` pins the parameter down, because a request that quietly stopped asking would raise no warning ever again and look no different.

`RatioPpm` is how many shares one share became in parts per million — a 2:1 split is `2_000_000`, a 1-for-5 reverse split `200_000`, an 8% stock dividend `1_080_000`. Parts per million rather than the provider's own numerator and denominator, for the reason a fee rate uses that scale: the ratio is frequently not a whole number, and one integer fine enough to hold every ratio either market declares saves every consumer its own division. **A ratio of exactly 1 is dropped** on the way in — the provider carries such an event occasionally, and a "split" that left every holding as it was would raise a warning about a book with nothing wrong with it, which is the fastest way to teach a reader to ignore the next one.

**Entitlement is decided by replaying the ledger to the session before the ex-date**, reusing the machinery the dividend prompt runs on (`sharesOn` over `ledgerShareRow`). The day *before* is the question, not the day itself: the ex-date is the first session trading in new shares, so an entry dated on it was already recorded in them, and counting it would warn a book that bought in afterwards and is perfectly correct. A holding **sold since is still warned about**, which is the opposite of what a "current holdings" check would do — the years it was held are in every report on `/reports`, and the split corrupted all of them.

**The warning is dismissible, and the dismissal is reversible.** `models.SplitAcknowledgement` is keyed by user where `SplitEvent` is not, because it is one reader's decision about their own ledger rather than a fact about the market. It has to exist: the condition that raises the warning — shares were held across that date — stays true forever no matter what the reader does, so a warning with no off switch is one everybody learns to scroll past. It has to be undoable for the opposite reason, since nothing else in the system will ever mention the split again. `POST`/`DELETE /reports/splits/ack` are the two halves, and acknowledging an event that is not stored is a **404 rather than a stored row that silences nothing**.

`GET /reports/splits` returns the unacknowledged ones; `?include_acknowledged=true` keeps the dismissed ones in the answer, flagged, which is what lets a holding's own page offer the undo. The frontend states the arithmetic and refuses to apply it: `splits.ts` says what a recorded count *became* ("1,000 shares recorded, which is 2,000 of today's stock"), because that is the correction a reader has to make by hand and the most this system can honestly offer. `formatSplitRatio` turns a reverse split around rather than printing `0.2:1`, which is true and reads as a rounding error.
