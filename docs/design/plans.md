# Savings plans

_Read before changing `RecurringPlan`, pending purchases, or the Savings Plans UI._

## Savings plans

`models.RecurringPlan` is a standing instruction to buy a fixed amount of one instrument on given days of the month — 定期定額. It is **configuration, not ledger**: a plan says what is *supposed* to happen and never that it did, and generates prompts, exactly as `DividendEvent` does. It is nonetheless edited **on the Ledger page** (`components/SavingsPlans.vue`, below the ledger table), not with the fee terms on `/account`: a missing or wrong instalment is noticed in the "Plan purchases not recorded" prompt at the top of that page, and the terms that raised it belong one scroll away rather than a page away. The component emits `changed` after every write so the page re-reads the prompts.

**An edit (`PUT /plans/:id`) changes the days, amount and start date in place, and never the instrument.** Instalments are matched to buys of the plan's instrument, so repointing one would orphan every purchase it already explained and raise a prompt for every date it has run — a different plan wearing this one's id, which is a delete and a new plan, the same rule that keeps an instrument's symbol and market fixed. The edit is a **correction and is retroactive**, since nothing about a plan is stored per instalment: every due date is recomputed from the new terms. That is safe for what is recorded — a buy answers an instalment by instrument and date, never by amount — so only the prompts still waiting move. A broker changing the plan *from a date onward* is a different event, modelled as Stop plus a new plan starting then, which keeps the earlier unrecorded instalments at their old amount; the edit row says so. A stopped plan cannot be moved to start after it stopped (`db.ErrPlanStartsAfterEnd` → 409).

**Nothing is ever posted automatically, and that is not squeamishness.** The system cannot know what an instalment filled at: the cash amount is fixed but the price is whatever the market did that morning, so the share count — the number every later figure is built on — is a guess until a statement says otherwise. `db.PendingPurchases` estimates it from the stored close on the due date and hands the trade form a filled-in draft; saving stays the user's act. `EstimatedShares` and `EstimatedPrice` are **nil, not zero**, when no close is stored — which is why `EarliestHistoryNeeded` counts a plan's start date, why **Record on an instalment with no estimate syncs price history first** and re-reads the prompt before filling the form (the usual cause is a plan on an instrument whose history was never synced, and without the fetch Record filled only the date and looked as though it had done nothing — pressing Record is the caller asking for exactly that data, so it is not a background fetch), and why `recordPurchase` raises the form's touched flags only for the fields it actually filled. Raising them regardless left a blank field captioned "entered by hand", a form claiming the user had typed what they had not, with the fee profile's own estimate suppressed for the rest of the entry.

**Amount is what a plan fixes**, which is why it is stored rather than a share count. A savings plan debits NT$3,000; how much stock that buys is an outcome.

`DaysOfMonth` is a canonical ascending list ("5,15,25"), normalized on write so two plans on one schedule cannot read as different ones. **A day past the end of a short month clamps to that month's last day rather than being skipped** — a plan for the 31st is a plan to buy monthly, and dropping February would quietly make it eleven purchases a year, which a reader would only notice by counting.

An instalment is answered by a buy in the same instrument dated within `purchaseClaimWindow` (10 days), and **each buy answers for at most one**: without that, a monthly plan recorded once would look fully kept all year. It is the same rule, for the same reason, as the dividend prompt. Ending a plan keeps it (`ended_on`) rather than deleting it, because the purchases it prompted are still in the ledger and the plan is the only record of why they are spaced as they are.

Fractional shares are what make this recordable at all: $500 a month into VOO at $708 buys 0.706 shares, and rounding that to 1 would put a 40% error into the cost basis on every instalment.
